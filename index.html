008 · FAQ

Questions clients ask first.

What is technical debt?

Technical debt is the extra cost you pay every time you change software that was built with shortcuts. Like a loan, it charges interest: each new feature takes longer, breaks more things and needs more people. You can design it out from the start, or pay it down in order, starting with whatever costs you the most.

What do I get from a Debt Check?

A one-week review of your codebase at a fixed price. You get a ranked list of what is costing you money and a plan to fix it. If you hire me for the cleanup afterwards, the check is credited in full.

How long does it take to build an MVP?

Most builds go from brief to production in 4 to 8 weeks. FreeMyTime took 2 to 3 weeks, the LuminaVera 3D configurator took 6 weeks, and the Studio Botema ERP was live in about 6 weeks. After launch, systems grow module by module, without a rewrite.

How much does a project cost?

Every project is scoped and quoted on its own. Builds are fixed price after scoping, with scope, price and date in writing before any work starts. The Debt Check is a fixed price, and Architect on call is billed monthly and can be cancelled anytime.

Which technologies do you use?

Proven, boring tools, so the next developer is productive on day one: React and Next.js on the front end, Node and Python behind it, PostgreSQL for data, three.js for 3D, Stripe for payments, and machine learning or LLMs where they remove real manual work.

Can you work with my existing team and codebase?

Yes. The Debt Check starts from the code you already have, and Architect on call puts a senior architect next to your team a few days a month for design reviews, tough technical calls and hiring help.

Do you add AI to existing products?

Yes, when it pays for itself. PropIntel uses a machine learning model to estimate property prices in minutes instead of hours of manual work. AI goes where it removes real work, not where it only looks good in a demo.

Do you work remotely?

Yes. Projects run remotely and in English, with short loops, working software and decisions made on real screens instead of long meetings.
